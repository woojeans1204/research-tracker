An [OpenReview page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5w04DhjZEa) exists, but its public review/discussion text was not accessible in this run; the oral acceptance is a strong positive venue signal, while citation traction is not yet meaningful for a paper this new.

### Recommendation

This is the diffusion-language-model paper I would read today. Pivot-SD exploits a signal specific to masked diffusion trajectories: a few token commitments sharply reduce uncertainty over the still-masked positions, so training only those “pivots” can assign credit more precisely than full-sequence SFT or step-level RL. The evidence is unusually disciplined for a new paper: controlled budgets, multi-seed results, ablations against random and all-token supervision, explicit compute accounting, tests on LLaDA-8B and Dream-7B, and math/code transfer checks.

### Why it matters now

Diffusion-LM post-training is increasingly borrowing autoregressive SFT and RL recipes even though denoising commits tokens out of order. Pivot-SD turns that structural difference into a concrete, cheap learning signal: with 200 questions and four frozen-model rollouts each, it reports the best 256-token mean among its compared methods on MATH500, GSM8K, HumanEval+, and MBPP+, while taking 2.8 hours on two L40 GPUs versus 3.6–5.4 hours for budget-matched diffusion RL and 17.3–22.9 hours for the larger RL runs. The broader value is a testable claim about where learning signal lives in a diffusion trajectory, not merely another optimizer variant.

### Core idea

- Rank denoising commitments by the normalized drop they cause in predictive entropy over positions that remain masked, then retain only the top information-gain pivots.
- Replay each pivot in its original partially masked state; reinforce pivots from successful trajectories with cross-entropy and penalize failed-trajectory pivots with targeted unlikelihood.
- Keep the pipeline offline: sample trajectories once, supervise only 10 tokens per trajectory (under 4% of the 256-token budget), and avoid repeated online rollouts.

### Broader trend

This belongs to the move from AR-style, sequence-level post-training toward **trajectory-native credit assignment for diffusion LMs**. Recent work is converging on denoising states, reveal order, uncertainty collapse, and commitment-level actions as the right units for training and verification; Pivot-SD is a particularly clean offline version of that trend.

### Read skeptically

The experiments cover only two similarly sized masked-diffusion backbones, verifier-friendly math and code tasks, and block-local credit assignment. The coding unlikelihood weight is tuned on a held-out set, and several budget-sweep cells are single runs. Most importantly, the paper does not compare end-to-end against strong autoregressive post-training baselines, and its rollout setup commits one token per denoising step, so it does not show that the resulting model realizes a practical parallel-decoding or serving advantage. The 512-token HumanEval+ result also falls below the base model, warning that length transfer is not uniformly robust.
