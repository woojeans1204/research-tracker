## 08:06 KST - A Tale of Two Temperatures: Simple, Efficient, and Diverse Sampling from Diffusion Language Models

- **Paper:** [A Tale of Two Temperatures: Simple, Efficient, and Diverse Sampling from Diffusion Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09921)
- **OpenReview:** [COLM 2026 page](https://openreview.net/forum?id=mUyvTuvWOZ)
- **Domain:** Diffusion language models
- **Venue signal:** Accepted at COLM 2026
- **Priority:** High
- **Topic tags:** diffusion-language-models, masked-diffusion, diffusion-inference, sampling-diversity, test-time-compute, RL-post-training
- **Broader trend:** Moving DLM progress from model architecture alone toward decoding policies that explicitly manage the quality, diversity, and compute tradeoff.

### Recommendation

This is the diffusion-LM paper I would read today because it identifies a specifically diffusion-shaped failure mode: confidence-based parallel decoding can preserve pass@1 while collapsing the branching diversity needed for pass@k, test-time search, and RL rollouts. The proposed fix is training-free, simple enough to reproduce, and evaluated on two open 7B-8B dLLMs across math and code, with comparisons to autoregressive decoding, deterministic confidence decoding, and random unmasking. Its COLM 2026 acceptance is a meaningful external validation signal, while fresh citation counts are not yet a useful discriminator.

### Why it matters now

The field is rapidly optimizing dLLMs for fewer denoising steps, but speed is not enough when the application needs multiple genuinely different candidates. This paper cleanly separates token temperature from a new position temperature and shows that the order in which masked positions are committed is itself an exploration control. That makes the work relevant to inference-time scaling and post-training, not just decoding benchmarks.

### Core idea

- Add a position temperature that stochastically softens confidence-based choices about *where* to unmask, separately from token temperature, which controls *what* token to sample.
- Instantiate this as Tempered Low-Confidence (TLC) and Tempered Confidence Thresholding (TCT), retaining parallel generation without retraining the model.
- Evaluate diversity and compute jointly: TLC largely restores autoregressive-like pass@k, while TCT improves pass@NFE and produces more useful, varied GRPO rollouts under matched wall-clock budgets.

### Broader trend

This belongs to a shift from treating the diffusion sampler as a fixed implementation detail to treating token-order policy as a first-class part of the language model. The important comparison is increasingly not just dLLM versus autoregressive accuracy, but the Pareto surface over single-sample quality, diversity, number of function evaluations, wall-clock cost, and downstream utility.

### What to read skeptically

- The fork-token theory depends on an idealized anchor-fork model and an assumption that the learned model preserves the relevant fork structure; it explains the proposed mechanism but does not establish a general theory of semantic diversity.
- The evidence is limited to LLaDA-8B-Instruct and Dream-7B-Instruct on GSM8K, MATH-500, HumanEval, and MBPP, so transfer to larger models, open-ended generation, and other corruption processes remains unproven.
- The advantage is metric- and selector-dependent: TCT can trail autoregressive sampling on raw pass@k, and at high compute it underperforms AR with self-consistency even though it improves the frontier with an outcome reward model. The paper also reports that downstream TCT and deterministic Fast-dLLM can be comparable despite a clearer pass@NFE gap.
