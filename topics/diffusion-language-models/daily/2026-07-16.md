### Recommendation Summary
PoE-Bridge is worth reading today because it attacks the central practical weakness of diffusion language models: parallel decoding is attractive, but quality still falls behind strong autoregressive models when token dependencies matter. The paper proposes an inference-time bridge between a DLM proposal and an AR target, with experiments on math and code tasks showing large gains over standard DLM decoding while retaining much of the parallel-decoding advantage.

### Why This Paper Matters Now
The current DLM literature is shifting from "can diffusion generate text?" toward "where does diffusion actually win after latency, verification, and AR baselines are accounted for?" This paper is useful because it treats AR scoring as a strength rather than a strawman baseline, and it makes the tradeoff explicit: DLMs draft in parallel, while an AR expert verifies and corrects enough structure to recover most of the AR model's task performance.

### Core Idea
- Use the DLM as a parallel proposal distribution and the AR model as a quality/faithfulness target.
- Insert a Product-of-Experts bridge distribution so correction happens in two easier stages rather than one brittle DLM-to-AR jump.
- Combine speculative rejection sampling, importance sampling, mixed-temperature candidates, and elastic rejection windows to improve both acceptance rate and compute use.

### What To Be Skeptical About
- The method is not a pure DLM win: it depends on access to a strong AR verifier/target, so the deployment story depends on whether AR scoring cost is acceptable.
- The strongest evidence is on math and coding benchmarks with Dream-7B and Qwen2.5-family pairings; open-ended generation and mismatched model families may behave differently.
- Some sampling approximations and finite-candidate importance resampling introduce bias, so the claimed faithfulness should be read as empirical rather than exact.
