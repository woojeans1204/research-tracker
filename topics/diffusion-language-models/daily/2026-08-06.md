## Mask-Aware Policy Gradients for Diffusion Language Models

- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15200
- Domain: diffusion language models
- Source: arXiv, submitted 2026-07-16; accepted at COLM 2026
- Recommended by: agent
- Priority: high
- Topic tags: diffusion-language-models, masked-diffusion, reinforcement-learning, diffusion-inference, reasoning, code-generation
- Broader trend: post-training and RL methods that treat the diffusion denoising trajectory as the policy, not just the final text sample.

### Recommendation summary
This is the diffusion LM paper I would read today because it attacks one of the central open problems in RL-tuning masked diffusion LMs: the generation process chooses both token values and the positions that survive remasking. The paper formalizes that as a two-stage action MDP and adds a masking-position term to the policy gradient, then tests it against strong recent dLLM RL baselines on math and code tasks. It is very new, so citation traction is not yet meaningful, but the COLM 2026 acceptance, code release, direct ablation against a token-only trajectory baseline, and wall-clock training-efficiency comparison make it more substantial than a typical frontier preprint.

### Why it matters now
- Diffusion LMs are moving from pretraining and decoding tricks into post-training, reasoning, and code-generation territory.
- The paper clarifies that unmasking/remasking order is part of the learned policy, not a harmless sampler detail.
- It gives a concrete way to compare ELBO-style, token-only, and trajectory-aware RL objectives under the same LLaDA-style setting.

### Core idea
- Model masked diffusion generation as two coupled decisions at every step: which tokens to predict and which positions to keep unmasked.
- Replace hard top-K remasking with a probabilistic Plackett-Luce-style position distribution so the unmasking choice contributes a differentiable log-probability term.
- Optimize token and masking gradients jointly, yielding consistent gains over GDPO, SPG, StepMerge, wd1, and related baselines on GSM8K, MATH500, HumanEval, and MBPP.

### What to read skeptically
- The result is still concentrated around LLaDA/Dream-style 7B-8B models, verifier-friendly math/code/planning tasks, and fixed generation-length settings.
- The method adds training complexity and extra forward-pass structure; the paper reports useful wall-clock comparisons, but deployment-time latency and general open-ended generation quality remain less settled.
- OpenReview signal is not yet inspectable from a stable forum page I could verify, even though COLM lists the paper among accepted papers and says accepted papers are hosted on OpenReview.
