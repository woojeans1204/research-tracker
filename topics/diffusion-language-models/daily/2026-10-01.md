## 08:06 KST — Why Deterministic PRM Guidance Underperforms in Discrete Diffusion Reasoning

- **Domain:** Diffusion language models
- **Date:** 2026-09-28
- **Venue / validation:** Accepted at NeurIPS 2026; the official NeurIPS 2026 listing corroborates the acceptance. No public OpenReview page was found, so review and discussion signals are unavailable. Citation traction is not yet meaningful for a paper this new.

### Recommendation

This is the diffusion-language-model paper I would read today. It asks whether the most natural form of process-reward guidance actually earns its extra inference cost, and answers with a careful matched-forward-pass comparison: on Dream-v0-Instruct-7B, independent sampling plus a task-matched outcome reward model beats deterministic PRM-guided pruning by 9.95 points on GSM8K at eight candidates, 9.85 points on MATH, and 12.16 points on MBPP. The durable contribution is not the negative headline but the decomposition of *why* guidance fails: weak signals on highly masked states destroy candidate diversity, while a scorer trained across mask ratios can also be a poor terminal selector.

### Why it matters now

dLLMs expose partially denoised full solutions, inviting researchers to import test-time search and process supervision from autoregressive reasoning. This paper shows that scattered masked states are not ordinary prefixes and that scorer calls, early pruning, and final verification must be evaluated as separate costs and mechanisms. It supplies a much-needed standard for judging the growing wave of reward-guided dLLM reasoning methods: compare against independent sampling plus a strong final verifier at matched compute, and measure whether guidance preserves the oracle ceiling.

### Core idea

- Train PRMs on intermediate dLLM snapshots, then compare deterministic top-1 guidance with independent sampling plus an outcome-reward reranker under nearly identical forward-pass budgets.
- Separate two failure modes: PRM ROC-AUC falls from 0.77 to 0.54 as masking increases, and early pruning cuts the attainable candidate-pool ceiling from 81.05% to 67.30% on GSM8K.
- Use SMC, top-M retention, final-state retraining, readout ablations, and a second backbone to show that diversity preservation and terminal-verifier specialization are distinct requirements.

### Broader trend

This belongs to the shift from treating diffusion's intermediate states as an automatic reasoning advantage toward **compute-matched, mechanism-aware test-time scaling**. The emerging lesson is that any-order denoising needs guidance and verification designed for partially observed, non-prefix states; AR-style PRMs cannot simply be transplanted unchanged.

### Read skeptically

The headline ORM advantage depends on task-matched verifier training; the paper's own out-of-distribution test shows a GSM8K-trained ORM fails to transfer to MATH500. The primary generator comparison is on Dream-7B, while LLaDA is used only for the causal-versus-bidirectional PRM check, not the full ORM-versus-guidance contest. The evidence covers math and code, binary outcome-supervised PRMs, one main sampling schedule, and a specific family of deterministic pruning algorithms, so it does not establish that stochastic, uncertainty-calibrated, step-supervised, or open-ended guidance must fail.
