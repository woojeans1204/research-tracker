## Diffusion Language Models: An Experimental Analysis

- Paper: [Bertolani et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19475), posted June 17, 2026.
- Domain: diffusion language models
- Broader trend: compute-aware evaluation of full-sequence versus block diffusion.

### Why this paper
A useful empirical reference for separating the potential of diffusion language modeling from the effects of decoding choices. It compares eight diffusion LMs across eight benchmarks and also examines smaller models trained on the same corpus. The authors' university repository lists a 2026 Italian Conference on Computational Linguistics publication, not a top-tier venue; external validation is modest, with two citations in the coverage of [one citation index](https://mlsanity.com/paper/2606.19475/) as of October 20, 2026.

### Core idea
- Evaluate full-sequence and block-based diffusion models alongside Qwen3 autoregressive baselines across reasoning, coding, translation, knowledge, and Sudoku.
- Vary denoising steps, generation length, block size, and parallel unmasking to expose quality-compute tradeoffs.
- Supplement large pretrained model comparisons with smaller architectures trained under common conditions.

### Critical notes
- Large-model comparisons are not controlled for training data or recipe; peak-score tables use each model's best configuration, not a common inference budget.
- Reported FLOP and memory measurements are not end-to-end latency or throughput; task and hardware affect practical speed.
- Results do not establish diffusion as the cause of Dream's Sudoku advantage over Qwen3, nor a general advantage over modern autoregressive serving.
