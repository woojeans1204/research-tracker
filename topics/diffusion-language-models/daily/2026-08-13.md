## Sumi: Open Uniform Diffusion Language Model from Scratch

- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19005
- Domain: diffusion language models
- Source: arXiv, submitted 2026-06-17; preprint
- Recommended by: agent
- Priority: high
- Topic tags: diffusion-language-models, uniform-diffusion, discrete-diffusion, language-modeling, scaling, open-models
- Broader trend: moving beyond masked diffusion toward natively pretrained, openly reproducible uniform-diffusion systems whose scaling and revision behavior can be tested directly.

### Recommendation summary
Sumi is the diffusion LM paper I would read today because it supplies a missing experimental control for the field: a fully open 7B uniform diffusion language model pretrained from scratch on 1.5T tokens, with weights, checkpoints, data-mixture specification, and training recipe. It is competitive with autoregressive models trained on comparable token budgets on knowledge, reasoning, and coding benchmarks, while reporting meaningful failures on commonsense tasks and little evidence that token revision produces genuine self-correction. Those negative results make the paper more durable than a simple model-release claim.

### Why it matters now
- Most scaled diffusion LMs use masked diffusion or AR-to-diffusion conversion, making it difficult to separate diffusion-family effects from inherited AR capability or small-scale training artifacts.
- Sumi provides a large, native uniform-diffusion reference point for fairer studies of scaling, generation order, controllability, and revision.
- Its evidence weakens a popular intuition: allowing any token to be revised does not automatically yield useful self-correction at inference time.

### Core idea
- Pretrain a 7B uniform discrete diffusion LM from scratch on 1.5T public-data tokens, allowing any position to change at any denoising step.
- Release weights, intermediate checkpoints, the full training recipe, and the data mixture so uniform diffusion can be studied as a reproducible scaling baseline.
- Compare against token-budget-matched AR and diffusion models, then probe parallel commitment and revision behavior rather than inferring benefits from likelihood alone.

### What to read skeptically
- The commonsense deficit is attributed partly to an education-heavy data mixture, but that explanation is not causally tested, so architecture and data effects remain entangled.
- The revision and self-correction probes use only 30 examples per task and are explicitly exploratory; the null result is important but not definitive.
- The paper does not yet establish a convincing deployment speed-quality frontier against a tightly controlled, scale-matched AR system, and generation degrades outside the trained canvas range.
- I found no stable OpenReview forum for this paper. The external validation signal is therefore limited: it is a recent arXiv preprint with open artifacts, but no mature review discussion or meaningful citation history yet.
