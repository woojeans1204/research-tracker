### Recommendation Summary
This is today's best diffusion-LM read because it attacks a central fork in the field: masked diffusion models tend to look better on understanding and zero-shot benchmarks, while uniform-noise diffusion models are often more attractive for few-step generation. XDLM proposes a mixed stationary-noise kernel that unifies those regimes and reports a better understanding/generation Pareto tradeoff, with an ICML 2026 spotlight signal and released training/evaluation code. It matters now because diffusion LMs are increasingly judged by practical speed-quality and controllability tradeoffs, not just whether they can imitate autoregressive likelihood metrics.

### Core Idea
- Treat MDLM and UDLM as endpoints of a broader mixed discrete diffusion family rather than as separate design camps.
- Use a stationary noise kernel plus posterior simplification to reduce the memory bottleneck and make the hybrid objective practical.
- Evaluate both understanding and generation behavior, including zero-shot text benchmarks, few-step generation quality, and an 8B LLaDA adaptation setting.

### Why Read Now
XDLM is useful as a design reference for deciding whether diffusion LMs should become more AR-like, more uniform-diffusion-like, or explicitly hybrid. The paper is not only a method proposal; it also frames the field's current bottleneck as a Pareto-front problem between semantic understanding and efficient generation.

### Skeptical Notes
- The strongest claims depend on whether the selected understanding and generation benchmarks fairly represent real deployment workloads.
- The 8B adaptation result is promising, but MBPP at 32 steps is still a narrow slice of coding and does not settle open-ended generation quality.
- Some evidence spans image-style generation metrics as well as language tasks, so separate the general discrete-diffusion contribution from the specifically language-modeling contribution.
