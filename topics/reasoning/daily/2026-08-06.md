## Conflicts Make Large Reasoning Models Vulnerable to Attacks

- Link: https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.463/

- Topic tags: reasoning, large-reasoning-models, safety, robustness, evaluation, chain-of-thought, interpretability
- Broader trend: moving from celebrating longer explicit reasoning traces toward auditing the new failure modes created by reasoning itself, especially safety failures that emerge inside multi-step deliberation.

### Recommendation Summary
This is the reasoning paper I would read today because it studies a failure mode that becomes more important as LRMs are deployed as deliberative agents: conflicting objectives can make safety behavior less stable. The paper evaluates three representative LRMs on more than 1,300 conflict-injected prompts across five benchmarks, then adds layerwise and neuron-level analysis to probe why safety and functional representations may interfere. It is not the flashiest method paper, but it is a durable diagnostic paper for anyone treating explicit reasoning as automatically safer or more controllable.

### Why It Matters Now
- Recent reasoning work has leaned heavily toward making models think longer, search harder, or verify more often; this paper asks whether longer deliberation also opens new attack surfaces.
- It gives a concrete test bed for conflict-driven failures rather than relying only on generic jailbreak prompts or multi-turn adversarial procedures.
- The ACL 2026 Findings publication, public code, multi-benchmark setup, and internal-analysis component make it stronger than a typical one-off safety demo, even though citation evidence is still naturally early.

### Core Idea
- Construct harmful-query settings where LRMs face internal value conflicts or dilemma-style contradictions, then measure how much those conflicts increase attack success.
- Evaluate multiple reasoning-oriented models, including Llama-3.1-Nemotron-8B, QwQ-32B, and DeepSeek R1, across over 1,300 prompts from five benchmarks.
- Use layerwise and neuron-level analyses to argue that conflict prompts shift and entangle safety-related and task-functional representations.

### What To Read Skeptically
- Check whether the conflict templates are genuinely representative of real user-agent conflict, or whether the effect depends heavily on prompt construction.
- Inspect the harmfulness and attack-success evaluation pipeline carefully, since classifier-based safety evaluation can introduce its own artifacts.
- Treat the representation analysis as suggestive mechanism evidence, not definitive proof that the same internal interference explains all reasoning-model safety failures.
