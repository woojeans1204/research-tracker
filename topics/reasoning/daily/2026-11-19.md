## 2026-11-19: SLR: Automated Synthesis for Scalable Logical Reasoning

- Paper: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.16/
- Domain: LLM reasoning; logical-reasoning evaluation and training
- Publication: ACL 2026 main conference, July 2026. The original preprint appeared in June 2025; the conference publication is within the six-month window.
- Topic tags: reasoning, reasoning-benchmarks, logical-reasoning, training, curriculum-learning, verification

### Recommendation
SLR is worth reading for its combination of a controllable reasoning benchmark and an actual training intervention. It synthesizes inductive logic tasks with executable checks, then studies model failures, difficulty and curriculum training rather than assuming that longer chains imply better reasoning. Its ACL main-conference publication and public implementation offer meaningful validation, though citation strength was not independently verified.

### Core idea
- Generate a latent logical rule, examples, prompt and executable validator from a configurable task specification.
- Evaluate models on 19,000+ prompts in 20 levels that vary relational, arithmetic and recursive complexity, distinguishing valid syntax from correct inference.
- Train on a difficulty curriculum; compare base and tuned models on SLR-Bench and outside tasks, with matched-budget curriculum-order ablations.

### Why now and broader trend
This provides a concrete example of verifiable synthetic environments for measuring and training reasoning, beyond test-time scaling. The paper reports that Llama-3.1-8B's logical reasoning level rises from 3.8 to 8.7 and that transfer varies across benchmarks; the matched-budget ordered-versus-random-versus-reversed curriculum comparison is especially useful for evaluating the causal story. See https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.16.pdf.

### Skepticism
Synthetic inductive logic and Prolog-output requirements are narrower than real-world reasoning; check whether failures stem from program syntax, the generated distribution, or actual induction. Training and evaluation share the SLR framework, so prioritize truly external tasks and effect sizes: LogiQA improves only 0.9 points in the main table. The ordered-curriculum ablation covers levels 5-7, not the entire curriculum. Inference-cost comparisons across differently sized and trained models do not establish training-inclusive compute efficiency.
