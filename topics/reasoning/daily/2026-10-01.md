### Recommendation summary
This is the reasoning paper I would read today because it tests whether reasoning survives contact with realistic agent workflows rather than another clean math prompt. AgentGym2 evaluates 15 proprietary and open models on 437 tasks across 27 domains, with tool discovery, data analysis, deep search, noisy inputs, and underspecified goals; even GPT-5 reaches only 46.15 Avg@3. The paper is unusually well validated for a recent benchmark: ACL 2026 main-conference acceptance, released code and data, controlled ablations, three runs per task, and 98.9% human agreement with its automated judge.

### Why it matters now
The field is moving from chain-of-thought accuracy toward agents that must plan, gather evidence, compose tools, recover from ambiguity, and finish an end-to-end job. AgentGym2 shows that scaling and extra interaction alone do not close this gap: stronger agentic post-training helps, but more turns are not a reliable scaling axis, and test-time sampling improves best-case success while worsening consistency. That makes the benchmark useful for deciding whether a new agent method improves robust reasoning or merely performs well in an idealized harness.

### Core idea
- Replace preselected task-specific tools with a basic composable toolbox, forcing agents to discover tools and build workflows.
- Evaluate planning and reasoning inside 437 realistic tasks spanning complex tool use, raw-data analysis, and noisy deep search.
- Isolate difficulty sources with controlled ablations for hidden information, tool discovery, and ambiguity/noise, then analyze cost, interaction depth, consistency, and failure modes.

### Broader trend
AgentGym2 belongs to the shift from static answer benchmarks toward de-idealized, process-oriented evaluation of agentic reasoning. The important move is not simply making tasks longer: it tests whether planning, exploration, tool composition, verification, and robustness remain coordinated when the environment does not hand the model a clean interface and complete specification.

### What to be skeptical about
The headline score is an end-to-end systems metric, not a clean measure of reasoning. Failures can originate in search, file handling, tool discovery, execution, or reasoning, and the benchmark only partially disentangles them; the 437-task suite is also expensive enough that prompt and hyperparameter ablations are limited. Its use of live public web interfaces improves realism but can make exact reproducibility and longitudinal comparison harder, while proprietary enterprise tools, safety, and security are outside scope.
