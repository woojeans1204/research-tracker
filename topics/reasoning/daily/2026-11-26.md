## AgentCoMa: A Compositional Benchmark Mixing Commonsense and Mathematical Reasoning in Real-World Scenarios

- Paper: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.380/
- Domain: LLM reasoning, compositional evaluation
- Publication: ACL 2026 main conference, July 2026. The original preprint appeared in August 2025: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19988
- Topic tags: reasoning, compositionality, evaluation, benchmark, agent

### Why read it now

AgentCoMa asks a sharp question for agentic systems: can a model combine commonsense selection and arithmetic when it can solve each component separately? Across 61 models, the paper reports a substantial compositionality gap, contrasted with human results and same-type compositional benchmarks. It is a useful diagnostic of reasoning reliability, rather than another inference-time scaling recipe; ACL publication is a strong validation signal, although citation strength was not reliably verified.

### Core idea

- Construct tasks in five everyday scenarios that require both commonsense and elementary mathematical reasoning.
- Evaluate each combined question alongside its component questions; compare combined accuracy with the proportion for which both components are answered correctly separately.
- Compare 61 models, human annotators, and same-type compositional benchmarks, then investigate possible causes with interpretability and contamination analyses.

### Broader trend

A shift from benchmark scores on isolated skills toward measuring whether models can compose skills reliably in agent-like workflows.

### Skepticism

The arithmetic is elementary and the tasks are static textual proxies, not interactive agents; a gap does not by itself establish a causal mechanism or general failure across all real-world workflows. Examine task construction, prompt and judging choices, human sample size, and sensitivity to decomposition or tool access. The study's evaluated models predate its conference publication, so current frontier-model performance should not be assumed. It is an evaluation paper, not evidence that additional inference cost is justified.
