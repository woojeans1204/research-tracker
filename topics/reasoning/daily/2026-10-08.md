## DeepPlanning: Benchmarking Long-Horizon Agentic Planning with Verifiable Constraints

- **Paper:** [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.335/) · [PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.335.pdf)
- **Authors:** Yinger Zhang, Shutong Jiang, Renhao Li, Jianhong Tu, Yang Su, Lianghao Deng, Xudong Guo, ChenXu Lv, and Junyang Lin
- **Domain:** LLM reasoning — agentic planning, tool use, and long-horizon evaluation
- **Venue:** ACL 2026, Volume 1: Long Papers
- **Published:** July 2026

### Recommendation

DeepPlanning is the strongest reading choice today because it tests a failure mode that short-horizon reasoning benchmarks largely miss: agents can satisfy many local constraints while still producing a globally invalid plan. Its multi-day travel and multi-product shopping tasks require active information gathering, local constraint handling, and joint optimization under time, budget, and cross-step dependencies, with rule-based verification rather than an LLM judge. The paper is especially useful as a diagnostic reference for separating convincing intermediate behavior from reliable end-to-end planning.

### Why It Matters Now

Agent evaluation is moving from isolated tool calls toward sustained workflows, but outcome claims are often blurred by local partial credit and harness effects. DeepPlanning supplies a concrete, reproducible target for global consistency: in the paper's evaluation, the strongest reported model reached only 44.6% average case accuracy, even while some component scores were much higher. The benchmark has also become an active substrate for follow-up planning methods, which makes it more durable than a one-off leaderboard.

### Core Idea

- Model practical planning as three coupled abilities: proactive information acquisition, local constrained reasoning, and global constrained optimization.
- Use travel and shopping environments with fine-grained, executable constraints so complete plans can be checked programmatically rather than graded by model preference.
- Diagnose the local-to-global gap: reasoning-enabled models improve, but small retrieval or constraint errors compound and global optimization remains the dominant failure mode.

### Broader Trend

This paper belongs to the shift from static chain-of-thought evaluation toward verifiable, long-horizon agent reasoning. The important change is not simply giving models more inference tokens; it is testing whether planning, tool use, state tracking, and final consistency survive across an extended trajectory. That makes DeepPlanning a useful counterweight to test-time-scaling work that reports better local reasoning without demonstrating reliable task completion.

### Read Skeptically

- The two domains are structured travel and shopping tasks, so generalization to open-ended software, research, or embodied agents is not established.
- Performance mixes model reasoning with tool-query policy, prompt design, parallelism, and budget; the benchmark does not fully isolate which subsystem causes a failure.
- Claims favoring explicit reasoning and parallel tool use are empirical associations in a specific harness, not a clean causal demonstration.
- The benchmark required annotation corrections in its later v1.1 release, so versioning and exact leaderboard protocol matter when comparing results.
