## [2026-10-29] Learning to Self-Verify Makes Language Models Better Reasoners

- Paper: [Learning to Self-Verify Makes Language Models Better Reasoners](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26es.html)
- Domain: LLM reasoning
- Source: ICML 2026 / PMLR
- OpenReview: [Forum](https://openreview.net/forum?id=rSDj9EM1HV)
- Priority: high
- Topic tags: reasoning, verification, RLVR, self-correction, training
- Broader trend: treating verification as a separately trainable reasoning capability rather than assuming it emerges from better generation

### Recommendation

This is the strongest fit today because it turns the generation-verification gap into a concrete training question: solving better does not reliably make a model a better judge of its own work, while verification training can transfer back into stronger and shorter generation. It is especially relevant to the user's earlier interest in the verification-generation asymmetry, while also respecting the preference to diversify away from another test-time-compute paper. The work has strong validation signals: ICML 2026 acceptance, experiments across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks and three Qwen2.5 model sizes, controlled comparisons among generation-only, mixed, staged, and alternating objectives, and released code.

### Why it matters now

Reasoning research is moving from simply rewarding correct final answers toward explicitly training the capabilities that make reasoning reliable: checking, detecting errors, and recovering from them. This paper offers evidence that verification is not merely a post-hoc filter; it can be a useful learning signal for the generator itself, potentially yielding better reasoning without requiring longer chains.

### Core idea

- Separate generation and self-verification inside the same RLVR framework instead of assuming one skill automatically produces the other.
- Train verification on model-generated candidate solutions labeled by rule-based outcome checks, then use either verification-first initialization or alternating generation/verification updates.
- Show a one-way transfer: generation training barely improves verification, but verification training improves generation efficiency and, when combined with generation training, raises average reasoning accuracy.

### Broader trend

The paper belongs to the shift from monolithic "reasoning RL" toward modular reasoning capabilities: verifier training, process supervision, error detection, and self-correction. Its distinctive claim is causal and training-side: verification should receive its own objective and budget, not merely be invoked as an inference-time prompt.

### What to read skeptically

- The main study uses Qwen2.5-Instruct models from 1.5B to 7B and is dominated by mathematical reasoning; transfer to frontier-scale models, open-ended tasks, tool use, and agentic planning remains unproven.
- Verification labels ultimately come from outcome-level rule-based checks, so better binary judgments do not guarantee faithful step-level diagnosis; a model may reach the right verdict for the wrong reason.
- The average gains from integrating verification are real but modest and uneven across model sizes and individual benchmarks; inspect per-task results rather than relying on the headline transfer story.
- The extra rollout, labeling, and alternating-training cost is not free, so the accuracy-token improvement at inference should be weighed against training compute.
